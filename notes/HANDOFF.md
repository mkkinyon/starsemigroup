# Handoff: regular *-semigroups — two projects (M. Kinyon with Claude, September 2026)

This tarball contains everything produced in a research session on regular *-semigroups. It covers two
projects. Project A (finished, written up as a paper) is about **how many regular involutions a semigroup
can carry**. Project B (ongoing, written up as detailed research notes) is about **which regular
*-semigroups embed in partition monoids**. Everything below is stated so that a fresh session can use it
without access to the original conversation; theorem numbers refer to the two PDFs.

Conventions. A *regular *-semigroup* is a semigroup with a unary operation satisfying x** = x,
(xy)* = y*x*, xx*x = x. Its *projections* are the elements p = p² = p*; the set of projections is P(S).
For projections, θ_p(q) := pqp, and (P(S), θ) is the *projection algebra* (East–Azeef Muhammed,
arXiv:2301.04845; every projection algebra is P(S) for some regular *-semigroup, a theorem of Imaoka).
In Prover9/Mace4 files the binary operation is x * y := θ_y(x) = yxy. The natural order is q ≤ p iff
q = pqp. Two projections p, q are *matched* (we write p ⋈ q) if pqp = p and qpq = q; this is exactly the
*friendly* relation p 𝓕 q of East–Gray–Azeef Muhammed–Ruškuc (2025). Two projections *commute* iff
θ_p(q) = θ_q(p) (equivalent to pq = qp, since pq then lies in a group H-class and squares to its identity).
P_X denotes the partition monoid on a set X (P_n for X = {1..n}), with the usual diagram involution.

## Project A — Semigroups with several regular involutions (`paper/involutions.tex`, `.pdf`, 20 pp.)

Question (Kinyon): a finite regular *-semigroup (S, *) with a second regular involution + gives an
automorphism φ(x) = (x*)⁺ of some order k; must S contain a square band A×A with |A| = k on which the
two involutions differ?

Results (all proved in the paper; the markdown files `01-…08-*.md` and `two-inversions-full.md` are the
earlier working notes, superseded by the paper; `verify*.py` are brute-force checks of the claims on
small semigroups):

* Counterexample to the question as stated: A = {1,…,5}, S = A×A, (x,y)* = (y,x),
  (x,y)⁺ = (yβ, xβ⁻¹) with β = (1 2)(3 4 5): k = lcm(2,3) = 6 > 5. The obstacle is exactly that k is an lcm.
* The corrected statement (Theorem 5.2): ρ(e) := e⁺* e⁺ is a permutation of P*; ρ(e) = e iff
  e⁺ = e; φ and ρ have the same order (no finiteness needed), k = lcm of the cycle lengths of ρ; every
  ρ-cycle (e_0 … e_{m−1}), m ≥ 2, gives a square band {e_a e_b} ≅ Z/m × Z/m, closed under both involutions,
  with (e_a e_b)* = e_b e_a and (e_a e_b)⁺ = e_{b−1} e_{a+1}, on which * and + differ. So the original
  question has a positive answer whenever k is a prime power (Corollary 5.3), in particular
  for k = 2 and k prime.
* k = 2 / commuting involutions (Theorem 3.1): coordinate-free three-line proof, no finiteness:
  distinct commuting regular involutions give a 2×2 square band {e, ef, fe, f} inside the
  {·,*,+}-subalgebra generated by any a with a* ≠ a⁺.
* Description of ALL regular involutions of a finite regular *-semigroup (Theorem 6.1):
  each is x⁺ = ρ⁻¹(x*x) x* ρ(xx*) for a unique permutation ρ of the projections, and a permutation ρ
  works iff φ_ρ(x) = ρ⁻¹(xx*) x ρ(x*x) is an automorphism; so |Inv(S)| ≤ |Aut(S)|. ρ preserves D-classes
  and the natural order of projections. Locally (on a completely 0-simple principal factor
  M⁰[G; I, I; P] in normal form) the condition is a Hankel-type condition on the sandwich matrix
  (Theorem 4.4, parametrisation in Corollary 4.5); globally there is a local + gluing
  decomposition (Theorem 6.6).
* Semigroups with a unique regular involution ("rigid"): need not be inverse (a 5-element band is the
  smallest example); rigidity is generic among completely simple regular *-semigroups with ≥ 3 R-classes;
  the Brauer, partial Brauer and partition monoids are rigid (Corollary 7.5, using
  Mazorchuk's description of their automorphisms).

## Project B — Embeddability in partition monoids (`embed/notes-embedding.tex`, `.pdf`, 27 pp.)

Question: which regular *-semigroups *-embed in some partition monoid P_X? Known before this session
(previous paper of Kinyon et al.): the necessary condition

  (Q1)  r ≤ p and r ≤ q  ⇒  pq = qp   (projections with a common lower bound commute),

equivalently (Prop. 2.1 of the notes): for every projection p, {x : px = xp = p} is an inverse
semigroup; equivalently an idempotent above a projection is a projection.

### Main results of the notes (theorem numbers as in the current PDF)

1. **Local structure is unconstrained** (Thm 3.1): every finite completely simple regular *-semigroup
   M[G; I, I; P] (any group, any sandwich matrix) embeds in a finite P_n, by an explicit construction
   with k|I|² points (k = degree of a faithful permutation representation of G). So groups and sandwich
   matrices impose no condition; all obstructions found live in the projection algebra.

2. **Matching-pattern (digraph) conditions** (Section 4). For a digraph E on [k], the *pattern
   condition* is the quasi-identity: if a_1..a_k pairwise commute with common product w, b_1..b_k
   pairwise commute with common product z, a_i commutes with b_i for all i, and a_i ⋈ b_j for every
   edge (i,j) ∈ E, then w = z. The simplest instances are the **triangle** E = {12, 23, 31} and the
   **hexagon** E = all six pairs. Theorem 4.2: the pattern condition holds in every P_X for every
   "admissible" E; Theorem 4.11: for finite X it holds iff E is *not a star*; Theorem 4.14 (**master
   condition**): in every projection algebra the triangle condition implies every non-star pattern
   condition, hence all non-star conditions hold in every P_X, and the path and 2-cycle conditions
   are equivalent to the triangle. The hexagon condition is equivalent to the triangle condition on
   finite projection algebras (Thm 4.9, a walk/Dedekind-finiteness argument) but not in general: the
   "unrolled hexagon" H_∞ (Thm 4.17, an infinite projection algebra built from three e-chains and
   three f-chains with level-shifting matchings) satisfies the hexagon condition and violates the
   triangle condition. Open: does the hexagon condition imply the k ≥ 4 conditions in general?

3. **Q1 is not sufficient** (Thm 4.19): P_3/μ (103 elements; μ identifies each partition of rank ≤ 1
   with the rank-0 partition obtained by cutting its transversal block) and P_3/(μ ∨ λ_{S_2})
   (67 elements) satisfy Q1 and violate the hexagon condition; so does the 61-element *-subsemigroup
   Q_0 generated by the six rank-2 projections. Q_0 = D ∪ (Π_3 × Π_3): a 6×6 trace with trivial groups
   whose zero pattern is a 6-cycle of matched pairs, over a 5×5 square band. Its 11-element projection
   algebra P(Q_0) is not a subalgebra of any P(P_X).

4. **Q1 + triangle (+ all pattern conditions) is not sufficient either** (Section 5, new). The
   **graph semigroups** Q(G, ⊥): for a graph G = (V,E), a set B and ⊥: V → B, Q(G,⊥) = (V×V) ⊔ (B×B)
   with (p,q)(r,s) = (p,s) if q,r ∈ V and (q = r or qr ∈ E), else (⊥p, ⊥s); (x,y)* = (y,x). It is a
   regular *-semigroup (a Rees matrix semigroup over the trivial group with sandwich matrix
   "adjacency + identity", whose zero is split into the rectangular band B×B); its projections are
   V ⊔ B with θ_v(v') = v if v ⋈ v' else ⊥v, θ_v(m) = ⊥v, θ_m ≡ m; it satisfies Q1 iff ⊥ is a proper
   colouring. The counterexample 𝔔 := Q(path a–c–b–d, ⊥) with ⊥a = ⊥b = w, ⊥c = ⊥d = z has 20
   elements; its 6-element projection algebra 𝔓 = {a,b,c,d,w,z} has matched pairs a⋈c, b⋈c, b⋈d, w⋈z
   and all other θ-values "the bottom of the subscript" (θ_a(b) = θ_b(a) = w, θ_c(d) = θ_d(c) = z,
   θ_a(d) = w, θ_d(a) = z, θ_a(z) = θ_b(z) = w, θ_c(w) = θ_d(w) = z, θ_w ≡ w, θ_z ≡ z). 𝔓 satisfies
   Q1, the triangle and hexagon conditions and every pattern condition tested (even the ones that
   fail in P_2), but embeds in no P(P_X). 𝔓 was found by enumerating all projection algebras of
   order ≤ 6 satisfying Q1 + triangle (Mace4 + isofilter: 2, 5, 16, 62, 296 of orders 2–6) and testing
   embeddability into P(P_m): all embed in P(P_4) (order ≤ 5) or P(P_5) (order 6) except 𝔓.

5. **The wiring condition** — the new necessary condition, a quasi-identity in the projection algebra:

   (S)  a ⋈ c, b ⋈ c, b ⋈ d, cd = dc, θ_a(cd) = θ_b(cd)  ⇒  a ⋈ d.

   In the binary notation: c*a=a, a*c=c, c*b=b, b*c=c, d*b=b, b*d=d, d*c=c*d, (d*c)*a=(d*c)*b ⇒ d*a=a
   (and a*d=d). Theorem 5.6: (S) holds in every P_X. Proof idea: with z = cd, Lemma 1.1(b) gives the
   block structure of commuting projections (c and d share their N-blocks except for "hubs": a union
   of T(c)-blocks that is a single N(d)-block, and vice versa); the joins ε_a∨ε_z = ε_b∨ε_z = ε_w∨ε_z
   coincide, so a and b share their N-blocks class by class (Step 3); in a "balanced" class the shared
   wiring forces each T(a)-block to pair with exactly one T(d)-block (Step 5, a hand argument); in a
   "hub" class one proves by hand that the number p of T(a)-blocks is ≤ 3, reduces the situation to a
   finite combinatorial object (touch types of wiring components + direct overlaps), and verifies the
   finitely many cases by SAT (`embed/hubtypes.py`; unsatisfiable for p ≤ 6, three solvers, < 1 s).
   The weaker variant (W) = Theorem 5.3 (add the hypothesis θ_a(d) = θ_a(z), conclude a = b, z = c)
   has a complete hand proof (Steps 1–6). Prover9 proves (S) ⇒ (W) in every projection algebra;
   Mace4 shows (W) + Q1 + triangle ⇏ (S) via a 9-element algebra 𝔓_9 (Example 5.7; 𝔓 with a middle
   layer m ⋈ n, m ⋈ k inserted, θ_a(d) = m strictly between w and a), which therefore is a second
   non-embeddable algebra, caught by (S) but not by (W). Q1, triangle and (S) are pairwise independent
   (P(Q_0) satisfies Q1 and (S) but not the triangle; 𝔓 satisfies Q1 and triangle but not (S); Q(K_2, constant ⊥)
   satisfies (S) but not Q1).

6. **Computations** (Section 6): all regular *-semigroups of order ≤ 8 (1310 classes of order 8 with
   nontrivial involution) embed in P_5 iff they satisfy Q1; so up to order 8, Q1 is necessary and
   sufficient. The smallest counterexample to "Q1 + triangle sufficient" has order between 9 and 20.
   The *-congruences of P_4 give nothing new (their zero-free quotients contain those of P_3).
   Up to order 6 the projection algebras embeddable in some P(P_X) are exactly those satisfying Q1,
   triangle and (S).

7. **General remarks** (Section 7): for finite S, embeddability is decided by the quasi-identities of
   the class {P_X} (an ultraproduct argument, Prop. 7.1); P_X embeds in the involuted semigroup of
   binary relations on 2^X via α ↦ R_α = {(A,B): A ⊔ B' is a union of blocks} (Prop. 7.2), and its
   relations are exactly the Boolean subalgebras of 2^X × 2^X containing (X,X).

### Open questions (Question 7.3 of the notes)
* Is embeddability of a finite regular *-semigroup determined by its projection algebra?
* Smallest counterexample to Q1 + triangle (order 9–20)? Hand proof of the hub case of (S)?
  Further wiring-type obstructions (longer paths, two commuting pairs)? Which coloured graphs (G,⊥)
  give embeddable Q(G,⊥) (all with ≤ 3 vertices do; the 4-vertex path with colouring w,z,w,z is the
  first failure)? Is the quasivariety generated by the P(P_X) finitely based?
* Conceptual description of the pattern conditions; the infinite case (hexagon ⇒ k ≥ 4 patterns?).

### What is proven vs. computer-verified vs. conjectural
Proven by hand: Theorems 3.1, 4.2, 4.9, 4.11, 4.14, 4.17, 4.19, 5.3, Lemma 5.1, Props 7.1, 7.2.
Proven with a finite computer verification as the last step: Theorem 5.6 (condition (S)).
Computer facts: the enumerations of Section 6; non-embeddability of 𝔓 in P(P_m), m ≤ 8, and of 𝔓_9 for
m ≤ 8 (both also follow from Theorem 5.6); independence of the three conditions.
Conjectural: nothing is currently conjectured to be sufficient; the natural guess is a finite list of
forbidden coloured-graph configurations, i.e. finitely many quasi-identities.

Note for a talk: the most presentable pieces are (A) the corrected band theorem and the k = 2 proof,
the parametrisation of involutions and rigidity of diagram monoids; (B) the completely-simple
embedding construction, the triangle condition as master condition with the 61-element witness Q_0,
the H_∞ example separating hexagon from triangle, and the 20-element graph semigroup 𝔔 with the
wiring condition (S) — the picture "T-blocks wired through shared N-blocks" explains both the
pattern conditions and (S).
