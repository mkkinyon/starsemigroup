## 1. The state of the problem as I understood it

The question is which finite projection algebras embed in some P(P_X). The previous read covered the proved conditions ((Q1), triangle, (S), (R), (R'), (V), (P1), (Q)) and the catalogue of verified-only ones. What is new is a change of scale: instead of isolated conditions, two infinite families indexed by the length n of a chain of commuting projections,

* (W_n): a ⋈ c ⋈ b, a chain c ~ x_1 ~ … ~ x_n ~ b of commuting projections, every x_i identifying a with c (θ_{x_i}(a) = θ_{x_i}(c)) ⇒ a ⋈ b; (W_1) is (R) with a and b renamed;
* (R'_n): the same chain without the last link x_n ~ b, closed instead by (R')'s auxiliary y ~ a, θ_{x_n}(b) = θ_y(b); (R'_1) = (R');

together with explicit witnesses P^ch_n (order 2n+5) and P^{R'}_n (order 2n+6) that violate (W_n), resp. (R'_n), satisfy the shorter members, and whose maximal proper subalgebras all embed (Proposition 5.37, for every n). The conditional Theorems 5.34/5.36 turn this into: if the chain conditions are valid in every P_X, the quasivariety generated by the P(P_X) has no finite basis, and the (W_n) (resp. (R'_n)) form a strictly increasing chain of conditions. The only missing piece is the validity of (W_n), (R'_n) in P_X, which is verified by SAT for small m and small n only; Section 5.19 records a serious but so far unsuccessful attempt at a uniform proof and argues that no "bounded local invariant" can carry it.

So the logical structure is now: **[validity of the chain families in P_X]** ⇒ [non-finite-basis theorem + an infinite list of minimal non-embeddable algebras], with the left-hand side open. The notes keep this conditional structure explicit almost everywhere; the exceptions are listed in 2.8.

A reformulation that I found useful and that is implicit in the notes (Section 5.19(g), the "normal form" c ≤ x_1 ~ x_2 ≥ z): since in P_X two projections commute iff they have a common lower bound ((Q1)), and since the set Id(a,c) = {y : θ_y(a) = θ_y(c)} of identifiers is a down-set (θ_z(a) = θ_z(θ_y(a)) for z ≤ y), the chain conditions say exactly:

> (W_∞) for every y in the connected component Comp(c) of c in the comparability graph of the poset Id(a,c), and every b ⋈ c with b ~ y: a ⋈ b.

The chain c ~ x_1 ~ … ~ x_n is a zigzag c ≥ m_0 ≤ x_1 ≥ m_1 ≤ x_2 ≥ … in this poset. For a finite projection algebra P, (W_∞) is equivalent to (W_|P|) (a chain with a repeated element can be shortened), so the family is a parametrised basis of the kind conjectured in Section 7, and membership is decidable by a breadth-first search. I use this formulation below both for computations and for the assessment of Section 5.19.

*"(R'') implies (W_2) in every P_X."* This is an evidence-level slip (the only substantive one I found in the new material). *Lemma A (candidate).* The statement is clear and the evidence level is stated. The two easy cases (x ≤ y, y ≤ x) are right. I did not find a proof of the incomparable case either; see 3.1 for what I think it needs.

*The evidence for (W_n), n ≥ 3.* By the authors' own observation in 5.19(g), every chain end in P(P_4) and P(P_5) is at distance ≤ 3 from c, so (W_n) for n ≥ 3 is a consequence of (W_3) *inside P(P_5)*. Hence "verified m ≤ 5 for n ≤ 4" (table, (W_n) row) carries no evidence for (W_4) beyond that for (W_3); the first informative test of (W_4) is m = 6, where radius 4 occurs. The same remark applies to (R'_n), n ≤ 3, at m = 5. The table should say so.

*New computations (mine).* With the harness `wsat.py` (variables not assumed distinct; each of the two conclusions θ_a(b) = a, θ_b(a) = b refuted separately; the machine was shared with the authors' own background jobs, so the times are pessimistic):

### 2.2 Section 5.15 (subsec:chainwit): Definition 5.33 and Theorem 5.34

*Definition 5.33* is complete (for x_i, "left" and "right" partition the other 2n+4 elements) and agrees with family.py (my transcription of the text reproduces its tables for n ≤ 8).

*P^ch_n is a projection algebra satisfying (Q1), for every n.* The notes leave this as "routine, not written" (checked by computer for n ≤ 6; I rechecked n ≤ 8). It is routine but short, and since the theorem is stated for all n it should be written. Here is the argument, with B the bottoms (θ_e ≡ e), T the tops, and for a top t: M_t := {y : θ_t(y) = t} (M_a = {a,c}, M_c = {a,b,c}, M_b = {b,c}, M_{x_i} = {x_i}; note y ∈ M_t ⇔ t ∈ M_y) and β_t(y) := θ_t(y) ∈ B for y ∉ M_t (β_a ≡ d, β_c ≡ e_0, β_b ≡ e_n, β_{x_i} = e_{i-1} on the left, e_i on the right). The one fact used repeatedly is

> (*) for tops t ≠ x_i, β_t maps every element to the same side of x_i as t itself (a, c, d, e_0 are left; b, e_n right; for x_j, j < i, the value e_j is left and for j > i the value e_{j-1} is right), and β_{x_i} maps left to e_{i-1} (left) and right to e_i (right).

(PA2) θ_x(θ_x(y)) = θ_x(y): trivial for x ∈ B or θ_x(y) = x; otherwise θ_x(β) = β for β = β_x(y), because d ∉ M_a, e_0 ∉ M_c, e_n ∉ M_b and e_{i-1}, e_i lie on the correct sides of x_i. (PA3) θ_x(θ_y(x)) = θ_x(y): trivial unless x, y are distinct tops with y ∉ M_x; then θ_y(x) = β_y(x) and θ_x(β_y(x)) = β_x(y) by (*) (for x ∈ {a,b,c} both sides are the constant β_x). (PA4) θ_x(θ_y(θ_x(z))) = θ_{θ_x(y)}(z): if x ∈ B both sides are x; if y ∈ B both sides are β_x(y); if y ∈ M_x the right side is θ_x(z), and the left side is θ_x(z) when z ∈ M_x (use y ∈ M_x ⇔ x ∈ M_y) and β_x(β_y(β_x(z))) = β_x(z) otherwise (constant β_x, or x = y = x_i and PA2); if y ∉ M_x the right side is β_x(y), and the left side is θ_x(β_y(x)) = β_x(y) (PA3) when z ∈ M_x and β_x(β_y(β_x(z))) = β_x(y) otherwise, by (*). (PA5) θ_yθ_xθ_yθ_x(z) = θ_yθ_x(z): put w = θ_y(θ_x(z)); if w = y then θ_x(z) ∈ M_y forces θ_x(z) = x ∈ M_y and θ_yθ_x(y) = θ_y(x) = y by PA3; if w ∈ B then β_y(β_x(w)) = w by (*), since w = β_y(t) with t ∈ {x} ∪ B on the same side of y as x. (Q1): θ_x(z) = θ_y(z) = z with x ≠ y forces z ∈ B below both x and y; the only tops sharing a bottom are consecutive chain members (e_0 ≤ c, x_1; e_i ≤ x_i, x_{i+1}; e_n ≤ x_n, b), and these commute (θ_{x_i}(x_{i+1}) = e_i = θ_{x_{i+1}}(x_i), etc.). ∎

*P^ch_n violates (W_n) and satisfies (W_{n-1}), for every n.* The violation is as stated. For the satisfaction (claimed by computer for n ≤ 6) a uniform argument: the identifiers of (a, c) in P^ch_n are c, the x_i and the bottoms; the commuting graph on them is the path c – x_1 – … – x_n with e_0 adjacent to c, x_1, e_i to x_i, x_{i+1}, e_n to x_n (and b); the only elements commuting with b are b, x_n and e_n, at distance n, n+1 from c; so no chain of length n−1 exists. The other unclosed 2-path (b, c, a) gives nothing (only c and the bottoms identify b with c, and they do not commute with a's partners a, d). I also checked by hand that P^ch_n satisfies every (R'_j) (the partners of a are a, d, and θ_{x_j}(b) ∈ {b, e_j} is never d), so "satisfies all other conditions" is proved at least for the (R'_j). My brute force confirms for n ≤ 5: exactly one violating instance of (W_n), two of (W_{n+1}), none of (W_j), j < n, none of any (R'_j).

*Theorem 5.34* is correct as stated and the proof is complete given Proposition 5.37. Irreducibility: θ_x(y) is a top only when it equals x (Definition 5.33: θ_a(c) = a, θ_c(a) = θ_c(b) = c, θ_b(c) = b, all other values bottom), so a top lies in a subalgebra only as a generator; a k-generated subalgebra with k ≤ n+2 omits one of the n+3 tops and lies in the maximal subalgebra "omit that top", which is closed because omitting a top never removes a product. Variable counts: (W_n) has n+3 variables, P^ch_n has n+3 tops, P^ch_{n+1} has n+4; so the last assertion ((W_{n+1}) not a consequence of (W_1), …, (W_n) and valid quasi-identities in ≤ n+3 variables) is covered by the count. Two small points: the conclusion about the quasivariety uses the projection-algebra analogue of Proposition 7.1 (same proof; it is not stated for projection algebras), and the parenthetical "both verified for small n" should read "the first verified for small m and n".

*Isomorphisms.* P^ch_2 ≅ candidate 6: consistent with the description of candidate 6 in 5.13 (d ↔ c, h ↔ x_1, i ↔ x_2, g ↔ e_0, c ↔ e_1, e ↔ e_2, f ↔ d). M.K.'s order-11 model (model11.json): my brute force confirms it is a projection algebra with (Q1), has 6 irreducibles, violates (W_3) in exactly one instance, satisfies (W_1), (W_2) and all (R'_j), and is not isomorphic to P^ch_3 (it has one more matched pair among the tops), as stated.

### 2.4 Section 5.17 (subsec:rpfam): Definition 5.35 and Theorem 5.36

*Definition 5.35* is complete and agrees with rpfam.py (n ≤ 8, my transcription). *A gap in the proof of Theorem 5.36 (last assertion).* The first assertion is fine (n+3 irreducibles, quasi-identities in ≤ n+2 variables, Proposition 5.37). The last assertion, "(R'_{n+1}) is not a consequence of (R'_1), …, (R'_n), of all (W_j), and of any valid quasi-identities in at most n+3 variables", is justified by "P^{R'}_{n+1} … satisfies all (W_j) and (R'_1), …, (R'_n) and has n+4 irreducible elements". But (R'_n) has n+4 variables (table: "4+n vars"), so the variable count covers only (R'_1), …, (R'_{n-1}); and the (W_j) with j > n have more than n+3 variables, while those with j ≤ n are covered only if they are valid, which is not among the hypotheses of 5.36. So "P^{R'}_{n+1} ⊨ (R'_n)" and "P^{R'}_{n+1} ⊨ (W_j) for all j" are asserted, not proved (the computer gives them for n+1 ≤ 5, j ≤ n+2). Both are true, by direct inspection, and the argument should be added:

> In P^{R'}_m the identifiers of (a, c) reachable from c by commuting steps are c, x_1, …, x_m, e_0, …, e_{m-1} (k does not identify: θ_k(a) = d ≠ e_0 = θ_k(c); e_m is adjacent only to b and to nothing that identifies), and the commuting graph on them is the path c – x_1 – … – x_m with e_i pendant between x_i and x_{i+1}; so x_m is at distance m from c. (W_j): the only elements commuting with b are b and e_m, neither reachable, so no instance exists for any j (for the other unclosed paths (c, b, x_m) and (x_m, b, c) the chain stays in {b, e_m} and must end at a partner of x_m resp. c, impossible). (R'_n), n < m: the partners of a are a, d, k with θ_y(b) ∈ {d, x_m}; the chain end x_n has θ_{x_n}(b) ∈ {b, e_i, x_m}, so an instance needs x_n = x_m and y = k, i.e. a chain of length ≥ m. ∎

(The chain-shortening remark of Section 1 also shows that "all (W_j)" for a finite algebra is the single condition (W_{|P|}), which is how the computer claim "all (W_j), j ≤ n+1" should be read.)

*The minimal-subset claim* ("exactly two minimal valid subsets of the 11 hypotheses at m = 4") and "no embedding for m ≤ 7" I did not rerun.

### 2.5 Section 5.18 (subsec:maxsub): Proposition 5.37

*Correctness.* Verified independently: for both families and all n ≤ 8 (120 maximal subalgebras), every product θ_y(x) of the subalgebra, computed from the block lists of chemb.make/rpemb.make with my Lemma-1.1(a) code, equals the table entry, and the maps are injective. The block lists printed in the text agree with the code in every case I compared (all eight cases). Point counts: 2n+1 for "omit x_i" (ch), 2n+2 for "omit x_i" and "omit k" (R'), 2n+3 otherwise, as stated.

*Two hand checks with Lemma 1.1(a).* (i) P^ch_1, omit x_1 (points α, β, γ; a = [α](βγ), c = [αβ](γ), b = [β](α)(γ), d = (α)(βγ), e_0 = (αβ)(γ), e_1 = (α)(β)(γ)): θ_a(c): the join ε_a ∨ ε_c = {αβγ}, U_K = {α}, K meets supp c, so [α](βγ) = a; θ_a(b): join {α | βγ}, U_{α} = {α}, K = {α} misses supp b = {β}, so (α)(βγ) = d; θ_c(d): join {αβγ}, U = {αβ} nontransversal, so (αβ)(γ) = e_0; θ_b(a): join {α | βγ}, U_{βγ} = {β} misses supp a, so (α)(β)(γ) = e_1; the remaining products go the same way and all agree with the table (θ_a(y) = d for y ≠ a, c; θ_c(y) = e_0 for y ∉ {a,b,c}; θ_b(y) = e_1 for y ≠ b, c; θ_e ≡ e). (ii) P^ch_2, omit x_1 (points α, β, γ, p, q): the pair {p, q} is a class of every join by itself; θ_{x_2}(y) is (α)(β)(γ) together with (pq) for y with the pair merged (a, c, d, e_0, e_1 — none of which has support meeting the pair) and with (p)(q) for b, e_2, i.e. e_1 resp. e_2, matching "left ↦ e_1, right ↦ e_2"; θ_y(x_2) for y ∈ {a, c, b} is the R-part of θ_y(e_j) with y's own pair part (d, e_0, e_2 respectively); and for y, x ∈ {a, c, b, d, e_j} the pair part of θ_y(x) is the pair part of y, which is consistent because θ_a, θ_c take values with merged pairs and θ_b with separate pairs. All agree.

*Is the "proof" acceptable?* The principle is correct and should be stated as a lemma: the point set is R ⊔ X_1 ⊔ … ⊔ X_n, no block of any listed projection meets two of these parts, hence ε_y ∨ ε_x is the disjoint union of the joins of the restrictions and θ_y(x) is computed part by part (this is the embedding P_R × ∏ P_{X_l} ≤ P_X). On a pair the restriction of each element is one of three things ([p][q], (pq), (p)(q)) and the pair rule in the text is then a three-line verification; on R the restriction of each element takes one of finitely many values ("R-types": e.g. in "omit x_i", left-type and right-type for the x_l and e_j), and the table entry's R-type is a function of the R-types of the factors. So the whole proposition reduces to a finite table of R-type combinations, all of which already occur for n = 3 (ch) resp. n = 4 (R'); the computer check at those n (or at n ≤ 7 as done) is then a proof for all n. As written, the proof is a sketch plus "verified n ≤ 7"; adding the half-page above (decomposition lemma, finiteness of R-types, the observation that n = 3/4 realises all combinations) turns it into a proof without writing out the routine list. I would do that rather than write the list.

### 2.6 Section 5.19 (subsec:joinstat)

*(a) Lemma 5.38* is correct. The proof uses (G1) to get ε_a ∨ ε_c, ε_b ∨ ε_c ≤ ε_a ∨ ε_b and then (G2) to exclude a second triple in a class; the converse of Lemma 1.1(c) used at the end is justified in 5.18 ("the converse holds since then pqp and p have the same blocks"). Numerically (P(P_4), all instances of a ⋈ c ⋈ b): 14259 instances satisfy (G1) ∧ (G2) and all have a ⋈ b. A useful negative fact: the weakening (G1_T) "every T(c)-block lies in one class of ε_a ∨ ε_b" together with (G2) does **not** imply a ⋈ b (1920 counterexamples in P(P_4)); the N(c)-blocks in (G1) are essential, because Γ_i ⊆ D needs the N(c)-blocks of Γ_i to stay inside D.

Is the reduction a simplification? Of the *closing step*, yes: it replaces the counting of Step 8 of Theorem 5.23 (and Step 9 of Theorem 5.16) by Lemma 5.38, uniformly. Of the *proof burden*, no: (G1) is an extra statement to prove, it is at present only verified (m ≤ 5; for (W_2) m ≤ 6), even for (R') where Step 8 is available for free from the identity y·ab = y·acb; and (G2) was the hard part of every existing proof. The sentence "Step 8 can be replaced by a proof of (G1)" is correct but should not be read as a simplification of the (R') proof. The alternative closing "(G2) + rank θ_a(b) = rank a" (which is what Step 8 delivers) is worth keeping in mind, since a rank identity may be obtainable algebraically.

*(b)* Fine; the conclusion "no proof of (W_2) can proceed by exhibiting one auxiliary and quoting (W_1)" is justified for a contracting z on the same point set plus one point.

*(c), (d)* Fine, with the correction to (T_n) below.

*(e) The statement (T_n) is wrong as printed.* With "=", (T_n) contains the inclusion ε_b ∨ ε_c ≤ ε_a ∨ ε_b, i.e. (G1), which is false without b ⋈ c: in P(P_4) take a = [0](1 2 3), c = [0 1 2 3], x = (0 1 2 3) (or x = [0 1][2 3]), b = [0][1 2 3]; then a ⋈ c, x ~ c, θ_x(a) = θ_x(c), x ~ b, but (ε_a ∨ ε_b) ∧ (ε_b ∨ ε_c ∨ ε_x) = {0 | 1 2 3} ≠ ε_b ∨ ε_c = {0 1 2 3}. Exhaustively in P(P_4), the equality fails in 34284 of the 178737 instances of the (T_1) hypotheses (none with b ⋈ c), in 66220 of 421335 for (T_2). So "unlike (G1), (G2) and a ⋈ b it does not use b ⋈ c" is false for the printed statement. What t1.py evidently checks, and what (d) quotes, is the one-sided version

> (T_n)^≤: (ε_a ∨ ε_b) ∧ (ε_b ∨ ε_c ∨ ε_{x_n}) ≤ ε_b ∨ ε_c,

and for this I find no failure: all 178737 / 421335 / 424491 instances for n = 1, 2, 3 in P(P_4), and 3.2·10^6 (n = 1) and 10^7 (n = 2) sampled instances in P(P_5), with b arbitrary commuting with x_n. The other "meet identities" in (e) and (h) are fine with "=" because there the right-hand side is trivially below the left. Correct the statement; the surrounding discussion ((T_n) as candidate invariant, (h)) is about the ≤ form and survives.

*(f)* The inference "any such decomposition verified in P(P_m) for a fixed m is an artefact of m" is a reasonable methodological warning, supported by two instances (m = 4 → 5, m = 5 → 6); it is not a theorem.

*(g) The unbounded radius.* I confirm the P_6 example (component of 234 identifiers, eccentricity 4, the printed geodesic). But "unbounded" is an extrapolation from 3 (P_5) and 4 (P_6): in P(P_7) I found eccentricity 4 and never 5, on 1572 random matched pairs with |supp| ≤ 2 and on eight hand-chosen generalisations of the P_6 example (e.g. a = (0 2 3 4 5)(1)[6], c = (0 3)(1)(2)(4)(5)[6]: 5210 identifiers, component 328, eccentricity 4). This is not strong evidence either way (the sampling is narrow), but the question matters more than the notes suggest: **if the radius of Comp(c) is bounded by some R in every P_X, then (W_R) implies every (W_n) in P_X, the uniform proof reduces to one condition, and the conclusion of (g) ("the proof must be carried by the chain") is wrong.** Since the partition ε_y can change along the chain only through transversal blocks (Lemma 1.1(b): merge T-blocks of y into one N-block of z, split an N-block of y into T-blocks of z, regroup T-blocks), and the T-blocks of an identifier are tied to the Γ-classes, a bound is not absurd; a construction with eccentricity ≥ 5 in P_7 or P_8, or a proof of a bound, should be the first thing tried (Section 4).

The second inference of (g), "the invariant of an inductive proof cannot be a property of the chain end alone in any bounded local language", is also not a theorem. What (g) shows is that *reachability from c* is not bounded-local. An inductive invariant need not characterise the component: the weakest one, Φ(y) := "y ∈ Id(a,c) and for all b ~ y with b ⋈ c, a ⋈ b", is a property of the chain end, and (W_∞) is exactly the statement that Φ is closed under the two kinds of steps (down to any z ≤ y, up to any identifier w ≥ z). Unbounded radius says nothing about whether some bounded-language superset of Comp(c) is closed under these steps. The notes' own (T_n), quantified over b, is of this kind.

*(h)* Fine. The remark that (G1), (G2) are verified under the full (W_2) hypotheses in P(P_6) is consistent with my direct (W_2) verification at m = 6.

## 3. Conceptual assessment

### 3.1 Is the direction of Section 5.19 a dead end?

Partly. What is sound: (G1) ∧ (G2) as the uniform target and Lemma 5.38 (clean and correct); the recognition that (W_2) does not reduce to (W_1) by contraction or by substitution into proved conditions; the corrected (T_n)^≤ as a chain statement that holds with an arbitrary partner b and hence is a candidate inductive invariant; and the methodological warning that m ≤ 5 clause-language invariants are unreliable. What I think is a dead end is the *search for the invariant in a bounded two-point clause language*, which the authors themselves have now given up, and the premature conclusion drawn from it that the invariant "must be carried by the chain". The notes have not yet tried the two cheapest things: (i) to settle whether the radius is bounded (2.6(g)); (ii) to formulate the induction on the *order* rather than on commuting chains. In the formulation of Section 1 the induction step splits into two one-step statements about a single identifier, "down" (Φ(y), z ≤ y ⇒ Φ(z)) and "up" (Φ(z), z ≤ w ∈ Id(a,c) ⇒ Φ(w)), and the up step is the only content. This is a cleaner object than "(T) for x_{n-1} with partners x_n, θ_{x_{n-1}}(b), x_{n-1}x_n" (5.19(e)), and it can be tested in P(P_5)/P(P_6) as a statement about two comparable identifiers z ≤ w plus the universally quantified b (with the usual caveat that a failure in P(P_m) is inconclusive because the quantifier over b is truncated).

On Lemma A (every block of c lies in one class of ε_y ∨ ε_a) the order formulation also helps: y ≥ z ≤ x with z = xy; c ~ x with x identifying gives ε_c ≤ ε_x ∨ ε_a (Step 5 of Theorem 5.23), z ≤ x is the easy case, and what is left is the up step z ≤ y with z, y identifying — exactly the obstruction. So Lemma A is the up step for the statement "ε_c ≤ ε_y ∨ ε_a", and proving it is the right warm-up.

### 3.2 Does an existing theory handle chains of commuting projections of unbounded length?

Not that I know of, in the form needed. In detail:

* *Chained projection groupoids (East–Azeef Muhammed; East–Gray–Azeef Muhammed–Ruškuc).* The chain groupoid is built from paths of *friendly* pairs (⋈), and its restriction structure is the order (down-steps). A commuting chain is a zigzag in the order, i.e. alternating restrictions and *co-restrictions* (going up to an identifier above a common lower bound); the framework has no operation for going up, and the hypothesis "y identifies a with c" is a statement about restrictions of a and c to y that is simply not preserved upwards. So the groupoid formulation gives a language for the hypotheses (θ_y(a) = θ_y(c) says the restrictions of a and c to y coincide, a ⋈ c says they are related by a transport) but not a tool for the up-steps. I would not switch to it now.
* *Biordered sets (Nambooripad).* Commuting idempotents e, f have S(e, f) = {ef}; E-chains in that theory are L/R-chains, again friendly, not commuting. No tool.
* *Commuting graphs of semigroups* (Araújo–Kinyon–Konieczny, "Minimal paths in the commuting graphs of semigroups", Europ. J. Combin. 2011; Araújo–Bentz–Konieczny on the symmetric inverse semigroup, Israel J. Math. 2015, and related work on diameters of commuting graphs). This is the literature for the radius question of 5.19(g) — the graph there is restricted to projections identifying a with c, but the technique (find "universal" or near-universal commuting elements; here the rank-0 projections, which commute with exactly the projections sharing their partition) is the same, and M.K. knows it. Note that rank-0 identifiers make the component "partition-graded": y ∈ Comp(c) iff the rank-0 projection with partition ε_y is, so the component is determined by a set of partitions and the radius question is about how far a partition can move by the three allowed moves.
* *Representation games (Hirsch–Hodkinson).* The right organising principle for an infinite basis of the kind now conjectured: the n-round game axioms are the natural "(W_n)", validity is automatic from the representation, and non-finite-axiomatisability is proved by exhibiting algebras on which ∃ survives n rounds — which is precisely what Theorems 5.34/5.36 do with P^ch_n. The analogy does not give a proof of validity of the (W_n) as written, because the (W_n) are not game axioms; but it suggests looking for a game whose n-round axiom *implies* (W_n), which would give validity of all (W_n) at once from the representation. I think this is the most promising theoretical route if the direct block proof keeps failing (Section 4(e)).
* *Lattices of equivalence relations* (permuting equivalences, n-permutability, Arguesian laws): commuting projections do not give permuting partitions, and the join calculus of 5.19(c) is a fragment of the partition lattice with the support data added; nothing transfers directly.

### 3.3 Two remarks on the families

* The explicit families are the strongest new result: Proposition 5.37 plus the hand arguments of 2.2/2.4 give, unconditionally, that P^ch_n and P^{R'}_n satisfy every valid quasi-identity in ≤ n+2 variables; so the *only* open point for the non-finite-basis theorem is the validity of (W_n) (or of (R'_n)) in P_X. Given that the (W_∞) formulation makes the family a decidable parametrised basis for finite algebras, the stakes are now: either the chain conditions are valid (non-finite-basis theorem, infinitely many minimal non-embeddable algebras of orders 2n+5, 2n+6) or some (W_n) fails in some P_X, in which case P^ch_n for that n embeds in some P(P_X) and the census methodology ("no embedding for m ≤ 7") has a false positive. Both outcomes are important, and the second one is not excluded by the present evidence for n ≥ 5 (for n ≤ 4 the m = 6 checks above are the first real evidence).
* The two families are "the same" family seeded by (R) and (R'), each adding two elements per link; one expects seeds from K0–K6 too (5.16(c) says so). Before building more families, the validity question for the first one should be settled, because a counterexample to (W_n) would reshape the whole list.
