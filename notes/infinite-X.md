# Infinite X: what the counting proofs really prove (18 Oct 2026)

Prompted by the external referee reports (ChatGPT I.1–I.2): the proofs of (Ab), (Hm) and
of the chain conditions (W_n) count classes of partitions and subtract, so they are proofs
for finite X only. This note records what was found when probing whether the statements
can be rescued for arbitrary X.

## 1. A quasi-identity valid in all finite P_m and in all finite projection algebras, false in P(P_N)

    (Phi_0)   a |><| c |><| b,  aba = a   =>   a |><| b .

Valid in every finite P_m: a |><| c |><| b gives rk a = rk b; aba = a gives
rk(bab) = #{classes of e_a v e_b meeting both supports} = rk(aba) = rk a = rk b, and
bab <= b of the same finite rank forces bab = b.

Valid in every finite projection algebra, without (PC): Lemma lem:descent(d) of the paper
(theta_c: down(a) -> down(c) is a bijection, theta_b: down(a) -> down(b) is injective with
left inverse theta_a because aba = a, equal sizes make it a bijection, and theta_a(b) = a
then forces b = theta_b(a)).

False in P(P_N). Points y, x_i, z_i, w_i (i >= 0):

    a:  T-blocks  A_0 = {y, x_0, z_0},  A_i = {x_i, z_i} (i >= 1);        N-blocks {w_i}
    b:  T-blocks  B_0 = {y, w_0},       B_i = {x_{i-1}, w_i} (i >= 1);    N-blocks {z_i}
    c:  T-blocks  C_i = {z_i, w_i};                                       N-blocks {y}, {x_i}

Joins: e_a v e_c has classes {y,x_0,z_0,w_0}, {x_i,z_i,w_i}: one A and one C each, so
a |><| c.  e_b v e_c has classes {y,w_0,z_0}, {x_{i-1},w_i,z_i}: c |><| b.  e_a v e_b has
classes K_0 = {y,x_0,z_0,w_0,w_1} containing A_0, B_0, B_1, and K_i = {x_i,z_i,w_{i+1}}
containing A_i, B_{i+1}.  Every class meeting supp a contains exactly one A and meets
supp b, so aba = a; but bab has the T-block B_0 u B_1, so bab != b.  (Checked on a window
of 30 indices, `scratchpad/hotel.py`; the structure is 1-periodic with classes of size <= 5.)
This is Hilbert's hotel: the pairing A_i <-> B_{i+1} through c and the pairing A_0 <-> {B_0,B_1}
through a v b are both "bijections" of infinite index sets.

Consequences.
* The quasi-identities of the projection algebras of finite partition monoids are strictly
  more than those of all P(P_X): "quasi-identity compression" is false, so no general
  transfer from finite to infinite X exists. (For the semigroup language this is even
  easier: s s* = 1 => s* s = 1 holds in every finite P_m and fails for the shift in P_N.)
* The subalgebra of P(P_N) generated by a, b, c above is infinite: v = bab merges B_0, B_1,
  theta_c(v) merges C_0, C_1, theta_a of that merges A_0, A_1, theta_b of that merges
  B_0, B_1, B_2, and so on. So this example does not refute the embedding form of
  compression (a finite P embeddable in some P(P_X) embeds in a finite P(P_m)), which
  remains open (Question q:decidable).

## 2. What is proved, and for which algebras

The block calculus (Lemmas lem:PX, lem:order, lem:commaux, the closing lemma) is valid for
every X. The counts occur only in:

* (Hm): the criterion "a |><| b iff rk theta_a(b) = rk a and ..." at the start; Step 1
  (rk theta_z(a) = rk a  =>  theta_a(z) = a); Step 6 (n < m).
* (Ab): Step 4 (two applications of Lemma lem:count, with subtractions m - n - t), Step 6.
* Section 6 (chain conditions): everything from Corollary cor:acomp on.

(A2), (A1), (X), (P1), (Q), (Sq), the digraph conditions, Theorem thm:cs and the graph
semigroups are count-free. (A2)'s "Step 8: counting" is a block comparison.

For the classification of finite projection algebras only validity in finite subalgebras
of P(P_X) matters, and there finite descent replaces rank:

* (Hm): Step 1 is Lemma lem:descent(c) applied in the finite algebra (this is exactly the
  proof of Corollary cor:K0pp); Step 6 is Lemma lem:descent(d). Steps 2-5 are block
  arguments. So (Hm), and (Hm'), hold in every finite subalgebra of every P(P_X), and in
  every finite P_m. Whether (Hm) holds in P(P_N) itself is open; the natural
  Hilbert-hotel attempt (the configuration of §1 with z, y added) runs into the
  commutation of y with c, see below.
* (Ab): Step 6 is lem:descent(d) again, but the two counts of Step 4 are global class
  counts (number of classes of e_a v e_b v e_c meeting supp c equals n + t; the E_i fall
  into exactly n + t classes of M) and have no obvious descent form. Open.
* (W_n): the invariant delta_{ac} is a global count; no descent form known. Open. (W_1) =
  (A1) is count-free via (A2).

## 3. Attempts at a Hilbert-hotel instance of (Hm) in P(P_N)

Starting from §1 (u = a, v = bab != b) one needs z with theta_b(z) = b, theta_z(a) = theta_z(b),
and y with yc = cy, theta_y(b) = v, theta_y(z) = u = a. From the algebraic Step 1 of the proof,
a <= y, v <= y and y commutes with a, b, c. A T(y)-block must connect B_0 and B_1 inside
A_0 (to make theta_y(b) = v); by yc = cy such a block lies in a class of type (i) or (ii)
of e_y v e_c, and either way it forces two T(c)-blocks into Gamma_0 or two T(b)-blocks into
one class of e_b v e_c. So this particular base configuration admits no y; a different
base (where the merging of B_0, B_1 is witnessed through chains rather than a single
block) has not been tried.

## 4. Options for the paper

(a) State (Ab), (Hm), (W_n) for finite X and for finite subalgebras of any P(P_X)
    (the latter is what Theorem 1.6 needs); state Theorem 1.5 for the quasivariety
    generated by the finite P_m; record Phi_0 as the reason the two quasivarieties differ.
(b) Find count-free proofs or Hilbert-hotel counterexamples for (Ab), (Hm), (W_n) in
    P(P_N). A search tool for eventually periodic partitions of N would be needed.
