# Paper outline: which projection algebras embed in a partition monoid?

Draft of 17 Oct 2026, for discussion. References "§x.y" and "Thm x.y" are to notes-embedding.tex, version of 17 Oct (90 pp.).
The aim is to decide what the paper claims, what it proves in full, and what it relegates to an appendix or to a
separate note. The notes are a record; the paper has to be an argument.

## 1. What the paper is about (one paragraph for the introduction)

A regular *-semigroup S embeds in a partition monoid P_X (as a *-subsemigroup) only if its projection algebra
P(S) satisfies certain quasi-identities, the first of which is the projection condition (Q1): projections with a
common lower bound commute. Which further conditions are necessary, whether finitely many suffice, and what the
smallest obstructions look like, are the questions. The answers: (i) necessary conditions come in infinite
families indexed by digraphs and by chain lengths, and the quasivariety generated by the P(P_X) is not finitely
based; (ii) local structure is no obstruction at all (every finite completely simple regular *-semigroup embeds);
(iii) the obstructions are all "wiring" phenomena -- a matched path a ⋈ c ⋈ b that the geometry of P_X forces to
close under side hypotheses on auxiliary projections -- and they can be classified completely up to order 8 (seven
minimal non-embeddable projection algebras, each with a proof) and, pending three algebras, up to order 9.

## 2. Proposed main results (the theorems the paper would state in the introduction)

Theorem A (local structure, §3, Thm 3.1). Every finite completely simple regular *-semigroup embeds in a finite
partition monoid; P_4 already contains non-orthodox completely simple *-subsemigroups.

Theorem B (digraph conditions and the master condition, §4, Thms 4.2, 4.14, 4.19). For every admissible digraph
E on [k] the condition (D_E) holds in every P_X; the triangle condition implies all (D_E) in every projection
algebra (finite case; the infinite case via the unrolled hexagon); the triangle condition fails in a 61-element
quotient of P_3 satisfying (Q1), and in no projection algebra of order ≤ 10 satisfying (Q1).

Theorem C (wiring conditions, §5, Thms 5.6, 5.16, 5.20, 5.23, 5.25, 5.29, 5.49, 5.50). The conditions (S), (R),
(V), (R'), (P_1), (Q), K4, K0 hold in every P_X; each is violated by an explicit projection algebra of order
≤ 8 which satisfies all the others and whose proper subalgebras all embed. Consequently the minimal
non-embeddable projection algebras of order ≤ 8 are exactly 𝔓 (order 6), 𝔓_7, and 𝔓_8^1, ..., 𝔓_8^7, and a
projection algebra of order ≤ 8 embeds in some P(P_X) iff it satisfies (Q1), (Tri), (S), (R), (V), (R'), (P_1),
(Q), (E_1), (E_6) [the last two being consequences of K0, K4 with (Q1), (W_2)].

Theorem D (chain family and non-finite basis, §5.14-5.20, Thms 5.45, 5.46). For every n the chain condition
(W_n) holds in every P_X (via the counting invariant, Lemma 5.40); the explicit witnesses 𝔓^ch_n of order 2n+5
satisfy (W_{n-1}) and every other condition on the list and violate (W_n); hence the quasivariety generated by
the P(P_X) is not finitely based and the embeddable finite projection algebras are not the finite models of any
finite set of quasi-identities.

Theorem E (graph semigroups: sufficiency, §5.4-5.5, Thms 5.8, 5.12). For graph semigroups Q(G,⊥) and their
layered generalisation, (Q1) and (S) are also sufficient.

Lemma F (finite descent, §5.22, Lemma 5.51). In a finite projection algebra, |↓p| is invariant along matched
chains; hence q ≤ p in one matched component forces q = p, and for a ⋈ c ⋈ b the three statements a ⋈ b,
θ_a(b) = a, θ_b(a) = b are equivalent. (This is the abstract shadow of the rank in P_X and belongs in the
preliminaries, since it is used to pass between K0 and K0''.)

Open at order 9: candidates 1, 2, 3 (§5.13). If they fall, Theorem C extends to order 9 with the additional
conditions (R'), (W_2), K0, K4, K0'' and whatever settles 1, 2, 3.

## 3. Proposed structure

1. Introduction. The problem, Theorems A-E in words, the shape of the answer (wiring), what is computational
   and what is proved (the three evidence levels, stated once and respected throughout).
2. Preliminaries. Projection algebras (Jones; Imaoka: every projection algebra is P(S)); P_X and the
   block calculus (§1, Lemma 1.1 = the single lemma everything rests on); matched pairs (= friendly relation
   of East-Gray-Azeef Muhammed-Ruškuc), the friends lemma 4.12; (Q1) and its reformulations (§2);
   finite descent (Lemma 5.51).
3. The local structure is unconstrained (§3). Short.
4. Digraph conditions (§4): the family (D_E), the triangle/hexagon equivalence (finite and infinite), the
   witness P(Q_0). This is self-contained and could be cut to the triangle condition alone if length is a
   problem; the unrolled hexagon (§4.2) is the part to shorten.
5. Wiring conditions I: (S) and (R), graph semigroups (§5.1-5.3, 5.6, with the sufficiency Theorems 5.8, 5.12).
   The hub case of (S) now has a computer-free proof via (Q), so no computer case remains in this section.
6. Wiring conditions II: the order-8 classification (§5.7-5.12, 5.21-5.22): (V), (R'), (P_1), (Q), K4, K0.
   Uniform presentation: path a ⋈ c ⋈ b, u = θ_a(b), v = θ_b(a), auxiliaries; the proofs by labels/cells.
   The K-table of §5.13 (K0-K6 and variants) goes here in abbreviated form; the dropped-hypothesis map is
   mentioned once as the method by which the proofs were checked.
7. The chain families and the non-finite basis (§5.14-5.20): the identification graph, the counting invariant
   (Lemma 5.40, Cor. 5.41), (W_∞), the witnesses, Theorem 5.46. (R'_n) stays as a remark with its witnesses
   and the statement that its validity is verified for small n only (unless the end step is proved first).
8. Independence and the status table (§5.12, 5.16): which conditions imply which, the violation matrix.
9. Computations (§6): enumeration of regular *-semigroups to order 8 and projection algebras to order 9,
   embedding searches, the census of candidates; as a section or an appendix.
10. Questions (§7, pruned to the ones still open).

## 5. Decisions needed

1. Scope: order-8 classification only (ready now) versus waiting for order 9 (candidates 1, 2, 3). My
   suggestion: write for order 8 now, with order 9 as a section "partial results and candidates"; if 1, 2, 3
   fall during the writing, promote.
2. Whether Theorem B (digraph conditions) stays in this paper or becomes its own short paper: it is logically
   independent of the wiring conditions and the two together make a long paper (my estimate: 45-55 pages
   without §4, 60+ with it).
3. The name and symbol of the matched relation: "friendly", F, following EGMR 2025, or keep ⋈ and "matched".
   Also notation θ_p(q) versus the EGMR binary notation.
4. Title. Candidates: "Projection algebras of partition monoids: necessary conditions and small obstructions";
   "Which regular *-semigroups embed in a partition monoid?"; "Wiring obstructions to embedding regular
   *-semigroups in partition monoids".
5. Journal/length target, which decides 2.

## 6. Writing plan

Phase 1 (can start now): preliminaries + Lemma 1.1 + descent; §3; the uniform presentation of the wiring
conditions with the eight proofs rewritten in one notation (the proofs of (R'), (P_1), (Q), K4, K0 are long and
were written at different times; they should share the vocabulary: classes Γ_i, Δ_i, cells, labels, bright/dark,
crossing blocks).
Phase 2: chain family and non-finite basis (the counting invariant section is in good shape).
Phase 3: computations and the census, independence table.
Phase 4: introduction last.

A reasonable first deliverable is a 10-page "Section 2 + Section 6 skeleton" with all theorem statements and
the proofs of K0 and (V) rewritten in the common notation, to settle the notation before the long proofs are
touched.
