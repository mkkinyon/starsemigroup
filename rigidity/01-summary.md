# Two inversions on a regular $*$-semigroup — summary

Throughout, $S$ is a regular $*$-semigroup, $+$ is a second involutory antiautomorphic inversion, and $\phi\colon x\mapsto x^{*+}$ (apply $*$, then $+$) is the resulting automorphism, of order $k$ when $S$ is finite. $P^*$ denotes the set of $*$-projections ($e=e^2=e^*$) and $P^+$ the set of $+$-projections.

## Short answer

**The conjecture is false as stated, but true after one correction; the correction is forced and is itself easy to describe.**

* **Counterexample.** Let $A=\{1,2,3,4,5\}$, $S=A\times A$, $(x,y)^*=(y,x)$ and $(x,y)^+=(y\beta,x\beta^{-1})$ with $\beta=(1\,2)(3\,4\,5)$. Then $\phi(x,y)=(x\beta,y\beta^{-1})$ has order $k=\operatorname{lcm}(2,3)=6$, but $|S|=25<36$, so $S$ contains no square band $A'\times A'$ with $|A'|=6$ at all. (The trouble is simply that $k$ is a least common multiple.) See `05-counterexamples.md`.

* **Corrected statement (proved in `03-main-theorem.md`).** Define, for $e\in P^*$,
$$\rho(e) \;=\; e^{+*}e^{+},$$
the unique $*$-projection $\mathcal L$-related to $e^+$. Then $\rho$ is a permutation of $P^*$, and:
  1. $\rho(e)=e \iff e^+=e$; hence $\rho=\mathrm{id}\iff {*}={+}$.
  2. $k$ equals the order of the permutation $\rho$, i.e. the lcm of the lengths of its cycles.
  3. For every cycle $(e_0\,e_1\,\dots\,e_{m-1})$ of $\rho$ with $m\ge 2$, the set
  $$E=\{e_ae_b : 0\le a,b<m\}$$
  is a square band, $e_ae_b\mapsto(a,b)$ being an isomorphism $E\cong\mathbb Z_m\times\mathbb Z_m$; it is closed under both inversions, with $(e_ae_b)^*=e_be_a$ and $(e_ae_b)^+=e_{b-1}e_{a+1}$ (indices mod $m$). These are distinct inversions on $E$, and $\phi|_E$ has order exactly $m$.

  So: *$S$ contains a square band $A\times A$ on which $*$ and $+$ restrict to distinct inversions, with $|A|=m$ for every cycle length $m\ge2$ of $\rho$, and $k=\operatorname{lcm}$ of these $m$'s.* In particular the original conjecture holds verbatim whenever $k$ is a prime power (then some cycle has length exactly $k$), so in particular for $k=2$ and $k$ prime.

* **Exactly when the original conjecture holds.** A $*$,$+$-closed square band $A\times A$ with $|A|=k$ and distinct restrictions exists iff there is a union $B$ of $\rho$-cycles with $|B|=k$, $\rho|_B\ne\mathrm{id}$, such that $\{ef: e,f\in B\}$ is a band (`03-main-theorem.md`, Remark). Each single cycle always works; unions of cycles need not.

* **The case $k=2$** has a completely coordinate-free three-line proof valid for *arbitrary* (not necessarily finite) regular $*$-semigroups, and confirms your hint: the bad $2\times2$ band lies in the $\{\cdot,*,+\}$-subalgebra generated by any $a$ with $a^*\ne a^+$. See `04-case-k-equals-2.md`.

Finiteness is used in exactly one place: to know that the principal factor of a $\mathcal D$-class is completely 0-simple (so that $\mathcal D=\mathcal J$ and Rees coordinates are available). Any hypothesis guaranteeing that (e.g. $S$ completely semisimple) would do; the $k=2$ argument needs nothing.
