# 4. The case $k=2$, coordinate-free (no finiteness needed)

Let $(S,*)$ be any regular $*$-semigroup and $+$ a second inversion with ${+}\ne{*}$ and $\phi^2=\mathrm{id}$, i.e. $x^{*+*+}=x$, equivalently $x^{*+*}=x^{+}$ for all $x$.

**Step 1: a $*$-projection moved by $+$.** By Fact 3 of `02-preliminaries.md` there is $e\in P^*$ with $f:=e^+\ne e$. (If $a^*\ne a^+$, one of $e=aa^*$ or $e=a^*a$ works, so everything below happens inside the subalgebra generated by $a$ — as you suggested.)

**Step 2: $f$ is again a $*$-projection.** $f$ is idempotent (anti-automorphisms preserve idempotents) and
$$f^*=e^{+*}=e^{*+*}=e^{+}=f,$$
using $e^*=e$ and $x^{*+*}=x^+$. Also $f=e^+\in V(e)$, so
$$efe=e,\qquad fef=f.$$

**Step 3: $\{e,ef,fe,f\}$ is a $2\times2$ square band.** Using only $e^2=e$, $f^2=f$, $efe=e$, $fef=f$:

| $\cdot$ | $e$ | $ef$ | $fe$ | $f$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $e$ | $e$ | $ef$ | $e$ | $ef$ |
| $ef$ | $e$ | $ef$ | $e$ | $ef$ |
| $fe$ | $fe$ | $f$ | $fe$ | $f$ |
| $f$ | $fe$ | $f$ | $fe$ | $f$ |

(e.g. $ef\cdot ef=(efe)f=ef$, $fe\cdot ef=f(ee)f=fef=f$, $ef\cdot fe=efe=e$). With $e\mapsto(1,1)$, $ef\mapsto(1,2)$, $fe\mapsto(2,1)$, $f\mapsto(2,2)$ this is exactly the table of $\{1,2\}\times\{1,2\}$. The four elements are distinct: $ef=e$ would give $fe=(ef)^*=e^*=e$ and then $f=fef=(fe)f=ef=e$; $ef=f$ would give $fe=f$ and $e=efe=(ef)e=fe=f$; $ef=fe$ would give $e=efe=(fe)e=fe=ef$, reducing to the first case.

**Step 4: closure and distinctness.** $(ef)^*=f^*e^*=fe$, so $*$ is the swap $(x,y)\mapsto(y,x)$. And $e^+=f$, $f^+=e^{++}=e$, $(ef)^+=f^+e^+=ef$, $(fe)^+=fe$, so $+$ is $(x,y)\mapsto(y\alpha,x\alpha^{-1})$ with $\alpha=(1\,2)$. These are the two distinct inversions of the $2\times2$ band. $\square$

**Remarks.**

* Nothing here used finiteness, so for $k=2$ the conjecture holds for all regular $*$-semigroups.
* The same argument works for any $2$-cycle $\{e,f\}$ of $\rho$ (see the main theorem), for arbitrary $k$: a $2$-cycle means $\phi^2(e)=e$, and Step 2 only needs $f^*=\phi^{-1}(e)=\phi(e)=f$.
* Compare with the general proof: in Rees coordinates $e=e_i$, $f=e_j$ with $\rho=(i\,j)$ on $\{i,j\}$, and the band is $\{e_{ii},e_{ij},e_{ji},e_{jj}\}$. A $2$-point block $\begin{pmatrix}1&p\\p^{-1}&1\end{pmatrix}$ is automatically flat, which is why no analogue of Lemma C is needed when $m=2$; for $m\ge3$ flatness of the orbit is a genuine consequence of $(\ast)$ and is where the work lies.
